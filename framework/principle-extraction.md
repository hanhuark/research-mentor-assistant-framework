# Principle Extraction

The framework should convert examples into reusable mentoring principles. The output should not be a scrapbook of examples; it should be a compact guide that explains how to judge new work.

## Extraction Questions

- What recurring issue does the mentor comment on?
- What does good work look like in this context?
- What failure pattern appears repeatedly?
- Is the principle universal, domain-specific, or mentor-specific?
- What evidence or reasoning justifies the principle?
- Can the principle be turned into a checklist, rubric, or review mode?

## Principle Categories

- Logic flow and paragraph structure.
- Citation practice and literature synthesis.
- Knowledge-gap framing.
- Methodology completeness.
- Modeling assumptions and justification.
- Baseline case and hypothesis-driven DOE.
- Plot design and figure discussion.
- Physical interpretation and mechanism comparison.
- Proposal significance and reviewer alignment.
- Slide logic, visual cleanliness, and presentation delivery.
- Research coding, reproducibility, and repository hygiene.

## Avoid Overfitting

Do not encode a mentor's surface style when the underlying principle is enough. Prefer "state the central topic in the first sentence" over "write exactly like this paragraph."

Do not turn one strong example into a universal rule unless it appears repeatedly or is supported by a clear rationale.
